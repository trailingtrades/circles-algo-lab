2

Do rules jo sab kuch badal dete hainThe two rules that change everything

Mudra

In sab mein do rules aise hain jo baaki sab se zyada mehenge hain, aur woh rate wale nahi hain.

Pehla — koi set-off nahi. Maan lo ek coin pe aapne ₹50,000 kamaaye aur doosre pe ₹50,000 gawaaye. Net zero, theek? Nahi. Aap ₹50,000 pe 30% tax denge, aur ₹50,000 ka loss bas gaya.

Aapka asli nateeja shoonya tha, aur aapne ₹15,000 tax diya. Kisi aur asset class mein aisa nahi hota.

Doosra — har sale pe 1% TDS. Woh profit pe nahi hai. Woh poore transaction value pe hai. Agar aap baar-baar trade karte ho, toh woh 1% baar-baar lagta hai — chahe aap haar rahe ho.

Two of those rules cost more than all the others, and neither is the rate.

First — no set-off. Say you made ₹50,000 on one coin and lost ₹50,000 on another. Net zero, right? No. You pay 30% on the ₹50,000, and the ₹50,000 loss simply disappears.

Your real outcome was zero, and you paid ₹15,000 in tax. No other asset class works like this.

Second — 1% TDS on every sale. Not on profit. On the whole transaction value. Trade often and that 1% applies again and again — even while you're losing.

Arth

Toh zyada trade karna yahan aur bhi mehenga hai?

Far more. We work it out fully in rupees in Chapter 46. For now, one line: here, frequency isn't just a cost — it's a tax event.

Jo suraksha yahan hai hi nahiThe protections that don't exist here

Mudra

Ab doosri cheez. Market 1 ke Chapter 1 mein Rahul Sir ne paanch stop dikhaye the — broker, exchange, clearing corporation, depository. Aur bataya tha ki har stop pe koi aapko bacha raha hai.

Yahan woh dhaancha hai hi nahi. Koi clearing corporation nahi jo aapke counterparty ki guarantee de. Koi depository nahi jahan asset aapke naam pe alag rakha ho. Koi investor protection fund nahi.

Aur VDA legal tender bhi nahi hai, aur securities bhi nahi. Toh securities market ke investor protection ke rules yahan lagte hi nahi.

Platform doob gaya toh kya hota hai? Iska koi saaf, pehle se tay jawab nahi hai — aur yahi is chapter ka sabse zaroori sentence hai.

Now the second thing. In Market 1's Chapter 1, Rahul Sir showed five stops — broker, exchange, clearing corporation, depository. And said someone protects you at each.

That structure does not exist here. No clearing corporation guaranteeing your counterparty. No depository holding the asset separately in your name. No investor protection fund.

VDAs are neither legal tender nor securities. So the securities market's investor-protection rules simply don't apply.

What happens if the platform fails? There is no clean, pre-defined answer — and that is this chapter's most important sentence.

M3–4M3–4~35 min~35 min
1

Ek register jise koi akela nahi badal saktaA register nobody can rewrite alone

Mudra

Blockchain ko samajhne ka sabse saaf tareeka yeh hai: woh ek register hai. Bas.

Sabse purana udaharan — gaon ka ek bahi-khaata jisme sab lenden likhe jaate hain. Dikkat yeh hai ki agar woh khaata ek aadmi ke paas ho, toh woh use badal sakta hai.

Blockchain ka jawab: us khaate ki copy sab ke paas ho, aur har nayi entry (block) pichhli entry se gani-tik roop se judi ho (hash). Ek entry badalne ke liye aapko uske baad ki saari entries badalni padengi, aur woh bhi sab ke paas ek saath.

Consensus woh tareeka hai jisse sab is baat pe raazi hote hain ki agli entry kya hogi. Alag chains ka consensus alag hota hai.

The cleanest way to understand a blockchain is this: it's a register. That's all.

The oldest example — a village ledger where every transaction is recorded. The problem is that if one person holds it, that person can alter it.

Blockchain's answer: everyone holds a copy, and each new entry (a block) is mathematically tied to the previous one (a hash). To change one entry you'd have to change every later one, on everyone's copy at once.

Consensus is how everyone agrees what the next entry is. Different chains use different consensus.

Arth

Yeh toh kaafi chalaak hai. Par isse asset valuable kaise ban jaata hai?

That's clever. But how does any of it make an asset valuable?

Mudra

Nahi banta. Aur yeh is poore market ka sabse zaroori jawab hai.

Technology ka achha hona aur asset ka valuable hona do alag cheezein hain. Email ek shaandaar technology hai. Email ka koi token nahi hai.

Jo log aapko technology samjha ke asset bechte hain, woh yahi do cheezein jaan-boojh kar mila dete hain.

It doesn't. And that is the most important answer in this entire market.

A technology being good and an asset being valuable are separate things. Email is a superb technology. Email has no token.

People who sell you an asset by explaining the technology are deliberately conflating those two things.

2

Teen alag cheezein, ek naamThree different things, one word

Mudra

Log “crypto” ek shabd ki tarah bolte hain. Usme kam se kam teen bilkul alag cheezein hain.

Ek — store-of-value ka dawa. Bitcoin ka case yahi hai: seemit supply, ek hi kaam. Us dawe pe aap sehmat ho ya na ho, par woh ek saaf dawa hai.

Do — ek programmable chain. Ethereum aur uske jaise: unpe applications chalte hain. Yahan asset ek fees ka token hai, na ki sirf ek store of value.

Teen — tokens ki lambi list. Hazaaron. Aur yahin survivorship bias ka poora khel hai.

People say “crypto” as if it were one thing. It contains at least three very different ones.

One — a store-of-value claim. That's Bitcoin's case: limited supply, one job. You may or may not accept it, but it's a clear claim.

Two — a programmable chain. Ethereum and its peers: applications run on them. Here the asset is a fee token, not simply a store of value.

Three — the long tail of tokens. Thousands of them. And this is where survivorship bias lives.

Survivorship bias — jo list aapko dikhayi jaati haiSurvivorship bias — the list you are shown

Aapko jo list dikhayi jaati hai — “in coins ne 100x diya” — woh bache hue coins ki list hai. Jo hazaaron coins launch hue aur mar gaye, woh us list mein hain hi nahi. Unka data aksar delist hone ke saath gayab bhi ho jaata hai.

Yeh wahi galti hai jo Market 1 ke Chapter 19 mein aaj ke index constituents pe 10 saal ka backtest chalane wali thi — bas yahan woh kahin zyada badi hai, kyunki yahan mrityu-dar kahin zyada hai.

Jo list aapko dikhayi ja rahi hai, uska sabse zaroori hissa woh hai jo usme nahi hai.

The list you're shown — “these coins did 100x” — is a list of survivors. The thousands that launched and died aren't in it. Their data often vanishes when they delist.

It's the same error as backtesting ten years on today's index constituents in Market 1's Chapter 19 — only far larger here, because the death rate is far higher.

The most important part of the list you're shown is what isn't in it.

Notice three separate things pulling the same way, each independent of the others.

One — fees. Every trade, both sides. Ordinary; every market has this.

Two — 1% TDS on full transaction value. Not ordinary. It attaches to turnover, not to profit. 40 sales means 40 applications.

Three — 30% with no offset. More trades means more separate gains and losses. Gains are taxed; losses are wasted. Frequency raises both — but tax lands only on the gains.

I don't know its intent. I know its effect. And the effect is: frequency carries a higher bill in this asset class in India than in any other.

Accumulation vs tradingAccumulation versus trading

Mudra

Iska seedha nateeja yeh hai: agar aap is asset class mein kuch karna hi chahte ho, toh rules-based periodic accumulation ka ganit active trading se behtar baithta hai — sirf is liye ki usme transactions kam hote hain.

Aur main ise recommendation ki tarah nahi keh rahi. Main sirf ganit bata rahi hoon. Kam transactions ka matlab hai kam fee, kam TDS events, aur kam alag-alag taxable gains.

The direct consequence: if you want to do anything in this asset class, rules-based periodic accumulation has better arithmetic than active trading — simply because it transacts less.

And I'm not saying this as a recommendation. I'm stating arithmetic. Fewer transactions means fewer fees, fewer TDS events, and fewer separate taxable gains.

But accumulation has its own risk, and it's large: you're buying steadily into something that can fall 80% and not return. Fewer transactions does not mean less risk.

Arithmetic answers one question: “which costs less?” It doesn't answer the other: “should this be done at all?” That comes in Chapter 51.

1

Ek coin nahi — poora universeNot one coin — a whole universe

Arth

Mudra, ab tak humne ek-ek coin ki baat ki. Par jinke paas system hai, woh toh saath mein sau coins dekhte hain. Woh kaam kaise karta hai?

Mudra, so far we've talked about one coin at a time. But people with systems watch a hundred at once. How does that work?

Mudra

Woh chunte nahi, rank karte hain. Yeh farak samajhna zaroori hai.

Chunna ek raay hai: “mujhe yeh coin achha lagta hai”. Rank karna ek niyam hai: poore universe ko ek hi maapdand pe number do, upar wale kharido, neeche wale chhodo ya short karo — aur apni raay ko beech mein aane hi mat do.

Isko cross-sectional kehte hain. Market 1 ke Chapter 23 mein aapne yahi equity pe dekha tha. Ganit wahi hai. Badalta sirf yeh hai ki yahan universe khud jhootha ho sakta hai.

They don't pick. They rank. That difference matters.

Picking is an opinion: “I like this coin.” Ranking is a rule: score the entire universe on one measure, take the top, drop or short the bottom — and never let your opinion in the middle.

It's called cross-sectional. You saw it on equity in Market 1's Chapter 23. The arithmetic is identical. What changes here is that the universe itself can be a lie.

Arth

Universe jhootha? Matlab?

The universe, a lie? Meaning?

Mudra

Matlab: jis list pe aap rank kar rahe ho, woh list hi galat tarike se bani hai. Us pe baad mein aate hain — pehle list banane ke niyam.

Meaning the list you're ranking on was built wrongly in the first place. We'll come to that — first, the rules for building the list.

2

Do cheezein jo backtest ko jhootha banati hainThe two things that make the backtest a lie

Vridhi

Arth, main equity se aayi hoon, par yeh do galtiyan har asset class mein ek jaisi hain — bas crypto mein inka asar bahut bada hai.

Pehli — survivorship aur delisting bias. Aap aaj ki listed coins ki list lete ho, uspe 2018 se backtest chalate ho. Jo coins beech mein mar gaye, woh list mein hain hi nahi. Toh aapka backtest sirf zinda bachne walon ka backtest hai.

Iska naap liya gaya hai. Ammann, Burdorf, Liebi aur Stöckl ka 2022 ka paper (Survivorship and Delisting Bias in Cryptocurrency Markets, SSRN 4287573) 2014–2021 ke 3,904 coins pe yeh naapta hai. Value-weighted portfolio mein bias saal ka 0.93% nikalta hai. Equal-weighted mein 62.19% saal ka.

Arth, I come from equity, but these two mistakes are the same in every asset class — they're just far larger here.

One — survivorship and delisting bias. You take today's list of listed coins and backtest it from 2018. The ones that died in between were never in the list. So your backtest is a backtest of the survivors only.

It has been measured. Ammann, Burdorf, Liebi and Stöckl's 2022 paper (Survivorship and Delisting Bias in Cryptocurrency Markets, SSRN 4287573) measures it across 3,904 coins from 2014 to 2021. In a value-weighted portfolio the bias is 0.93% a year. Equal-weighted, it is 62.19% a year.

Arth

Bahasath pratishat? Ek hi galti se?

Sixty-two percent? From one mistake?

Vridhi

Ek hi galti se. Aur dhyaan do ki dono number kahan se aate hain. Jo coins marte hain woh lagbhag hamesha chhote hote hain — isliye market-cap se weight karo toh bias chhota, aur sab ko barabar weight do toh bias bahut bada.

Aur retail machhli pakadne kahan jaata hai? Chhote naamon mein hi. Yani jo sabse zyada is bias ka shikaar hota hai, wahi sabse zyada is bias ke bharose backtest banata hai.

From one mistake. And notice where the two numbers come from. The coins that die are almost always the small ones — so weighting by market cap makes the bias small, and weighting everything equally makes it enormous.

And where does retail go fishing? In exactly those small names. So the person most exposed to this bias is the one most likely to be building on it.

Vridhi

Doosri — volume ka jhooth. Rank karne ka har filter volume pe tika hota hai. Par crypto ka reported volume bharosemand hai hi nahi.

Bitwise ne March 2019 mein US SEC ke saamne 83 exchanges ka analysis rakha: reported Bitcoin volume ka lagbhag 95% fake ya non-economic tha. Sirf 10 exchanges ka volume credible mila. Yeh 2019 ka naap hai, par sawaal aaj bhi wahi hai — jis volume pe aap filter laga rahe ho, woh kisi ne banaya toh nahi?

Two — the volume lie. Every ranking filter leans on volume. And reported crypto volume is not trustworthy.

In March 2019 Bitwise put an analysis of 83 exchanges before the US SEC: roughly 95% of reported Bitcoin volume was fake or non-economic. Only 10 exchanges showed credible volume. That is a 2019 measurement, but the question is unchanged — is the volume you're filtering on something somebody manufactured?

Factor zoo — aur yahan woh zyada khatarnak haiThe factor zoo — and why it bites harder here

Crypto mein cross-sectional factors pe asli research hai. Liu, Tsyvinski aur Wu ka paper (Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency, Journal of Finance, 2022) teen factors — market, size, momentum — se cross-section ko samjhata hai.

Par usi saal Ammann et al. ne dikhaya ki jab delisting returns ko shaamil kar lo, toh momentum aur market beta ka returns se positive rishta bacha hi nahi, aur size bhi kamzor pad gaya. Do peer-reviewed papers, ek hi saal, ek doosre ko kaat rahe hain.

Aur uske upar Harvey, Liu aur Zhu ki chetavni (NBER WP 20592) — unhone 316 published factors ginne ke baad kaha ki normal significance hurdle pe mile zyadatar factors jhoothe hone chahiye. Crypto mein history chhoti hai aur search space bada — toh yeh chetavni yahan kam nahi, zyada lagti hai.

Is chapter ka rukh isliye yeh hai: cross-sectional dhaancha seekhne laayak hai. Uska koi bhi nateeja maanne laayak nahi jab tak aap khud, mare hue coins ke saath, apne costs ke saath, use dobara na nikaal lo.

There is real research on cross-sectional factors in crypto. Liu, Tsyvinski and Wu (Common Risk Factors in Cryptocurrency, Journal of Finance, 2022) explain the cross-section with three factors — market, size and momentum.

But that same year Ammann et al. showed that once delisting returns are included, momentum and market beta no longer show a positive relation with returns, and size survives only in weakened form. Two peer-reviewed papers, one year, cutting against each other.

On top of that sits Harvey, Liu and Zhu's warning (NBER WP 20592): having counted 316 published factors, they argued most factors found at the usual significance hurdle must be false. Crypto has a shorter history and a bigger search space — so that warning binds harder here, not softer.

So this chapter's position is: the cross-sectional method is worth learning. None of its published results are worth believing until you reproduce them yourself, with the dead coins in, and your own costs applied.

3

Perpetual futures — jiski expiry hi nahiPerpetual futures — the contract with no expiry

Arth

Ab ek sawaal jo main tab se rok raha tha. Yeh “perpetual” kya hai? NIFTY future ki toh expiry hoti hai.

Now a question I've been holding back. What is this “perpetual” thing? A NIFTY future has an expiry.

Mudra

Perpetual future ki koi expiry date hi nahi hoti. Dated future expiry ke din spot se mil jaata hai — yahan woh ghadi hai hi nahi. Toh phir uska bhaav spot ke paas rehta kaise hai?

Funding se. Har kuch ghante ek payment hota hai. Agar perpetual ka bhaav spot se upar chal raha hai, toh long shorts ko paisa dete hain. Neeche chal raha hai toh ulta.

Kitne ghante — yeh har contract ka apna parameter hai, aur woh badalta rehta hai. Kahin 8 ghante, kahin 4, aur dabaav ke waqt 1 ghanta tak. Ise venue ki sthaayi khoobi mat samajhna.

Yeh paisa exchange ko nahi jaata. Woh ek trader se doosre trader ko jaata hai. Aur wahi woh rassi hai jo bina expiry ke bhi bhaav ko spot ke paas kheench ke rakhti hai.

Formula ka dhaancha aam taur pe ek jaisa hota hai: ek premium index (perpetual order book spot index se kitna hata hua hai) plus ek clamp lagaya hua interest component. Venue ke constants, clamp aur interval — teeno badalte rehte hain, aur announcement se badalte hain. Dhaancha yaad rakho; number jis din chahiye, us din venue ke apne docs se lo aur uspe taareekh likho.

A perpetual future has no expiry date at all. A dated future converges to spot on expiry day — here there is no such moment. So how does its price stay near spot?

Through funding. A payment happens every few hours. If the perpetual trades above spot, longs pay shorts. If below, the other way round.

How many hours is a per-contract parameter, and it changes. Eight hours in places, four in others, as short as one under stress. Don't treat it as a fixed property of the venue.

This money does not go to the exchange. It goes from one trader to another. That is the rope that keeps the price near spot without an expiry.

The shape of the formula is usually the same: a premium index (how far the perpetual's book sits from the spot index) plus a clamped interest component. A venue's constants, its clamp and its interval all change, and they change by announcement. Remember the shape; take the numbers from the venue's own docs on the day you need them, and write the date beside them.

Arth

Aur agar bhaav mere against chala jaaye?

And if the price goes against me?

Mudra

Yahan teen alag bhaav hote hain, aur yahi sabse badi galat-fehmi hai. Last traded price — jo screen pe dikhta hai. Index price — kai spot exchanges ka fair-value average. Mark price — index se nikala gaya.

Zyadatar bade centralised perpetual venues pe aapka unrealised PnL aur aapka liquidation mark price se nikalte hain, venue ke last traded price se nahi. Mark price banta kaise hai, yeh har venue apne rulebook mein likhta hai aur announcement se badalta hai. Isliye ek venue pe ek second ka wick liquidation ki sambhavna kam karta hai — khatam nahi.

There are three different prices here, and this is the biggest misunderstanding. Last traded price — what the screen shows. Index price — a fair-value average across several spot exchanges. Mark price — derived from the index.

On most large centralised perpetual venues your unrealised PnL and your liquidation are computed from the mark price, not the venue's last traded price. How the mark price is built is in each venue's own rulebook and changes by announcement. So a one-second wick on one venue reduces the chance of a liquidation — it does not remove it.

4

Tejas, 3 baje raat koTejas, at 3am

Tejas

Bhai, mera toh account hi saaf ho gaya. Bhaav wapas bhi aa gaya subah tak — par position tab tak thi hi nahi.

Brother, my whole account got wiped. The price even came back by morning — but by then there was no position left.

Mudra

Tejas, teen cheezein ek saath hui hongi. Ek — koi circuit nahi tha, kyunki yahan circuit hote hi nahi. Do — 3 baje raat ko depth patli thi, toh aapka liquidation khud bhaav ko aur neeche le gaya. Teen — leverage ne in dono ko guna kar diya.

Aur ek chauthi baat jo aapko dikhi bhi nahi: us raat aap funding bhi de rahe the. Woh har kuch ghante chupchaap aapke equity se nikal raha tha, aur wahi aapko maintenance margin ke thoda aur kareeb le aaya.

Tejas, three things happened at once. One — there was no circuit breaker, because there are none here. Two — at 3am the book was thin, so your own liquidation pushed the price further down. Three — leverage multiplied both.

And a fourth thing you never saw: that night you were also paying funding. It was leaving your equity quietly every few hours, and it walked you that much closer to maintenance margin.

Tejas kuch nahi kehta. Mudra screen band kar deti hai.

Tejas says nothing. Mudra closes the screen.

Mudra

Main aapko yeh nahi bata rahi ki size kam rakhte. Main yeh bata rahi hoon ki is instrument mein aapka nuksaan aapke stop pe nahi rukta — woh wahan rukta hai jahan engine ko khareedar milta hai. Woh do alag jagah hain.

I'm not telling you to size smaller. I'm telling you that in this instrument your loss does not stop at your stop — it stops where the engine finds a buyer. Those are two different places.

Offshore leverage — ek line saaf hai, baaki dhundhliOffshore leverage — one line is clear, the rest is fog

India mein VDA ka apna koi kaanoon hai hi nahi. Do hi cheezein hain: ek tax regime, aur ek anti-money-laundering regime. Beech mein jo khaali jagah bachti hai, use log galti se “allowed hai” samajh lete hain. Khaali jagah ijaazat nahi hoti.

FEMA mein crypto ka naam kahin nahi aata, aur RBI ne aaj tak koi clarification nahi di. Jo line har blog pe milti hai — “RBI ne LRS pe crypto ban kar diya” — uske peeche aaj tak kisi ne circular number nahi dikhaya. Woh bank ki purpose-code practice hai, published prohibition nahi. Is course mein hum use prohibition nahi kahenge.

Par ek line saaf hai, aur woh theek isi chapter pe girti hai. LRS ki prohibited list mein “overseas exchange ya overseas counterparty ko margins ya margin calls ke liye remittance” shaamil hai. Yeh crypto ka rule nahi hai — yeh remittance ka rule hai, aur ise is baat se matlab hi nahi ki neeche asset kaunsa hai. Yani offshore leveraged account mein margin bhejna ek alag sawaal hai, aur uspe kaanoon chup nahi hai.

Aur enforcement asli hai, chahe rule likha hua na ho. Enforcement Directorate ne crypto ke cross-border flows ko FEMA contravention maana hai — ek mamle mein FEMA section 37A ke tahat ₹289.68 crore ki assets seize hui (Government ka Lok Sabha mein jawab). June 2026 mein ED ne ₹2,500 crore se zyada ke FEMA violations ka aarop lagaya.

India has no VDA statute at all. There are exactly two regimes — a tax regime and an anti-money-laundering regime. The empty space between them is routinely mistaken for permission. Empty space is not permission.

FEMA nowhere mentions crypto, and the RBI has issued no clarification. The line you read on every blog — “the RBI banned crypto under LRS” — has never been backed by a circular number by anyone. It is bank purpose-code practice, not a published prohibition. This course will not call it one.

But one line is clear, and it lands exactly on this chapter. The LRS prohibited list includes “remittances for margins or margin calls to overseas exchanges / overseas counterparty”. That is not a crypto rule — it is a remittance rule, and it does not care what the underlying asset is. So wiring margin to an offshore leveraged account is a separate question, and on that one the law is not silent.

And enforcement is real even where the rule is unwritten. The Enforcement Directorate has treated cross-border crypto flows as FEMA contraventions — in one case seizing ₹289.68 crore of assets under FEMA section 37A (stated by the Government in the Lok Sabha). In June 2026 the ED alleged FEMA violations exceeding ₹2,500 crore.

  • Kya mera pichhla ya planned remittance jaayaz tha? — FEMA counsel se, likhit mein.
  • Agar pehle bhej chuka hoon toh compounding ka exposure kya hai? Penalty amount ka guna ho sakti hai.
  • INR-settled perpetual pe TDS lagta hai ya nahi? Kuch platforms “0% TDS” ka dawa karte hain. Woh dawa court mein kabhi test nahi hua. Ise na maano, na khaarij karo — CA se poochho.
  • Was my past or planned remittance lawful? — from FEMA counsel, in writing.
  • If I have already remitted, what is my compounding exposure? Penalties can be multiples of the amount.
  • Does TDS apply to an INR-settled perpetual? Some platforms market a “0% TDS” position. It has never been tested in court. Neither accept nor dismiss it — ask a CA.

Yeh teeno sawaal CA ya FEMA counsel ke liye hain, YouTube ke liye nahi. Aur teeno ka jawab likhit roop mein rakhna — wahi ek cheez hai jo baad mein aapke kaam aayegi.

All three are questions for a CA or FEMA counsel, not for YouTube. And keep all three answers in writing — that is the one thing that will help you later.

Funding goes to another trader, not the exchange — and PnL and liquidation are computed from the mark price, not the last traded price. ADL has no NSE equivalent.

Yeh kaam kahan aayega — practicallyWhere you'll actually use this
  • Jab koi crypto backtest dikhaye. Aap do sawaal poochoge: mare hue coins andar hain? Aur volume kis exchange ka hai?

  • Funding rate padhte waqt. Aap dekhoge ki aap de rahe ho ya le rahe ho — aur woh aapke holding period pe kitna jud jaata hai.

  • When someone shows a crypto backtest. You'll ask two things: are the dead coins in it, and whose volume is that?
  • When someone says “proven factor”. You'll ask whether it was reproduced with delisting returns included.
  • Reading a funding rate. You'll see whether you're paying or receiving, and what it compounds to over your holding period.
  • Thinking about leverage. You'll remember that liquidation stops at the order book, not your stop — and that a thing called ADL exists.
  • When someone says “this platform is FIU-registered”. You'll ask: that registration is for AML — which regulator examined this product? And if money is leaving the country, you'll remember the LRS line.

Ek rupaye ka gain — ghar kitna pahunchta haiOne rupee of gain — how much reaches the house

Rahul Sir

Ab ek ganit. Maan lo har jagah aapne theek ek rupaya kamaaya. Har jagah se ghar kitna pahunchta hai?

PPF aur EPF se poora rupaya. Equity ke long-term gain se 12.5% aur uspe 4% cess — yani 13% — toh 87 paise. VDA se 30% aur 4% cess — yani 31.2% — toh 68.8 paise.

Ab ulta sawaal poochho, kyunki asli sawaal wahi hai. VDA ko equity ke barabar paisa ghar pahunchane ke liye kitna zyada kamaana padega? 87 bataa 68.8 = 1.264. Yani lagbhag 26.5% zyada — sirf tax ke farak ki wajah se, aur koi farak ginne se pehle.

Now some arithmetic. Suppose you earned exactly one rupee in each of these. How much of it reaches the house?

From PPF and EPF, the whole rupee. From a long-term equity gain, 12.5% plus 4% cess — 13% — so 87 paise. From a VDA, 30% plus 4% cess — 31.2% — so 68.8 paise.

Now ask it backwards, because that is the real question. How much more must a VDA earn to put the same money in the house as equity? 87 divided by 68.8 is 1.264. About 26.5% more — purely from the tax difference, before counting any other difference at all.

Woh niyam jo tab bhi tax leta hai jab aapne kuch kamaya hi nahiThe rule that taxes you when you earned nothing

Ab woh cheez jiska equity mein koi jodidaar nahi hai. Maan lo saal bhar mein aapke do VDA positions the. Ek mein ₹1,00,000 ka fayda hua, doosre mein ₹1,00,000 ka nuksaan. Arthik roop se aapne kuch nahi kamaaya — shoonya.

Wahi do positions listed equity mein hoti, toh taxable gain shoonya hota. Yeh farak rate ka nahi hai. Yeh dhaanche ka farak hai, aur yeh aapke trades jitne zyada honge utna hi bada hota jaayega. Chapter 52 ismein hi ek bot jod dega.

Now the thing with no equivalent in equity. Say you had two VDA positions in a year. One gained ₹1,00,000, the other lost ₹1,00,000. Economically you made nothing — zero.

Under the VDA rules a loss cannot be set off against any income — not even against another VDA's gain. So tax applies to the full ₹1,00,000 gain: 30% = ₹30,000, plus 4% cess ₹1,200. ₹31,200 in total — on zero earnings. (Surcharge, where your income attracts it, sits on top.)

The same two positions in listed equity would leave a taxable gain of zero. This is not a difference of rate. It is a difference of structure, and it grows with the number of trades you make. Chapter 52 adds a bot to exactly this.

The most common argument is: “keep a little, it diversifies the portfolio.” I teach this asset class, and I have to put that argument honestly.

First — the evidence that exists is not Indian. The IMF's Global Financial Stability Note 2022/001 (January 2022) and IMF Working Paper 2023/213 (Iyer & Popescu, September 2023) both measure against the S&P 500, not the NIFTY. I could not find a single peer-reviewed study on the NIFTY.

Second — that evidence does not favour the diversification case. The IMF's 2022 note finds the crypto-equity correlation rose after the pandemic to exceed even equity-gold and equity-bond correlations. The 2023 paper goes further: crypto mostly transmits shocks rather than absorbing them.

Third, and this matters most: the correlation rises exactly when markets are falling. So the diversification disappears on precisely the day you needed it.

Section numbers badal chuke hain — aur hum unhe fact ki tarah nahi likhengeThe section numbers have changed — and we will not print them as fact

Ek baat jo is poore course ke liye zaroori hai. Income-tax Act, 1961 1 April 2026 se repeal ho chuka hai, aur uski jagah Income-tax Act, 2025 aa gaya hai. VDA ka matlab nahi badla — 30% flat, 1% TDS, koi set-off nahi, koi carry-forward nahi, sab wahi hai. Par section numbers badal gaye hain.

Iska matlab yeh hai ki donon numbering ek saath zinda hain. FY 2025-26 ka return purane Act ke tahat jaayega — wahan s.115BBH aur s.194S hi sahi citations hain. FY 2026-27 se aage naya Act lagta hai. Is course mein jahan bhi purane section numbers likhe hain, woh purane Act ke hain.

Naye numbers kya hain, iske liye kai tax-practice sources ek hi jawab dete hain. Par hum unhe yahan fact ki tarah nahi likh rahe, kyunki humne Act ka asli text khud nahi padha. Ek SEBI-registered firm ke liye galat statutory citation chhapna wahi galti hai jisse yeh course aapko bachana chahta hai.

Toh niyam yeh hai, aur yeh aapke liye bhi hai: provision ko uske kaam se pehchano, number se baad mein. “VDA pe flat 30% wala charge” aur “VDA transfer pe 1% TDS wala provision” — yeh do naam kabhi purane nahi honge. Number har baar CA se confirm karo, us saal ke liye.

Something this whole course needs you to know. The Income-tax Act, 1961 stands repealed from 1 April 2026 and is replaced by the Income-tax Act, 2025. The VDA substance has not changed — flat 30%, 1% TDS, no set-off, no carry-forward, all intact. But the section numbers have moved.

Which means both numbering systems are live at once. The FY 2025-26 return runs under the old Act, where s.115BBH and s.194S remain the correct citations. From FY 2026-27 the new Act applies. Wherever this course prints the old section numbers, they are the old Act's.

Several tax-practice sources give the same answer on what the new numbers are. We are not printing them as fact here, because we have not read the bare text of the Act ourselves. For a SEBI-registered firm, printing a wrong statutory citation is precisely the mistake this course exists to teach you to avoid.

So the rule, and it is for you as much as for us: identify a provision by what it does, and only then by its number. “The flat 30% charge on VDAs” and “the 1% TDS on a VDA transfer” will never go stale. Confirm the number with a CA each year, for that year.

Now look at how many things one automated trade actually triggers. In Market 1 an equity trade meant: an order, a fill, a contract note, and a capital-gains statement at year end. That was all.

Here every transfer carries 1% TDS — on the consideration, not on the gain. It is deducted at credit or payment, whichever is earlier. And most importantly: a crypto-to-crypto swap is not one transfer, it is two. Each side is selling something.

Who deducts was settled by CBDT Circulars 13/2022 (22 June 2022) and 14/2022 (28 June 2022). On an exchange trade the exchange usually deducts; peer-to-peer, the buyer does. Reporting is through Form 26Q, or Form 26QE for a specified person; the certificate is Form 16E. Note that both circulars were issued under the old 1961 Act. Whether the same guidance and the same form numbers survive under the new Act is a question for your CA.
