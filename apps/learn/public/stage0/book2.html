Rupaya Kaise Chalta HaiHow the Rupee Trades

Currency pair hota kya hai — aur woh ek rule jo is poore segment ka matlab badal deta hai.

What a currency pair is — and the one rule that changes what this entire segment is for.

M1–2M1–2~40 min~40 min
1

Amaan ki problemAmaan's problem

Surat. Ek textile export house ka office. Amaan ke saamne ek invoice hai.

Surat. The office of a textile export house. An invoice sits in front of Amaan.

Amaan

Arth, yeh invoice dekho. Hum ne aaj maal bheja hai. Paisa 90 din baad aayega — 2,40,000 dollar.

Aaj ke rate pe woh ek raqam hai. 90 din baad rupaya kahan hoga, mujhe nahi pata. Agar rupaya mazboot ho gaya, toh wahi dollar kam rupaye laayega — aur mera poora profit us farak mein chala jaayega.

Main rupaye pe bet nahi karna chahta. Main sirf yeh chahta hoon ki mujhe aaj hi pata ho ki 90 din baad kitne rupaye milenge. Bas itna.

Arth, look at this invoice. We shipped goods today. The money arrives in 90 days — 240,000 dollars.

At today's rate that's one number. Where the rupee will be in 90 days, I don't know. If the rupee strengthens, those same dollars bring fewer rupees — and my entire margin goes into that difference.

I don't want to bet on the rupee. I want to know today how many rupees I'll have in 90 days. That's all.

Arth

Aur currency derivatives isi ke liye hain?

And currency derivatives exist for that?

Amaan

Isi ke liye bane the. Aur ab, kaanoon ke hisaab se, lagbhag sirf isi ke liye hain.

That is what they were built for. And now, by law, that is close to all they are for.

Yeh rule pehle padho — baaki sab iske baad aata haiRead this rule first — everything else follows it

Januray 2024 mein RBI ne ek circular nikaala jo 3 May 2024 se lagu hua (pehle 5 April 2024 ki tareekh thi, jo aage badha di gayi). Uska matlab seedha hai: exchange-traded currency derivatives sirf ek valid underlying contracted exposure ko hedge karne ke liye use ho sakte hain.

Saari currency pairs milaakar USD 100 million ke barabar tak, participant ko exposure ka saboot dena zaroori nahi — par use is baat ki pushti karni padti hai ki aisa exposure maujood hai.

Iska practical matlab yeh hai ki yeh segment ek speculation ka maidan nahi hai. Yeh ek hedging ka auzaar hai. Is market mein hum use usi tarah padhate hain — kyunki woh kaanoon bhi hai aur sach bhi.

Apne broker ki current onboarding aur declaration process khud padho. Yeh rule implementation mein broker-dar-broker thoda alag dikhta hai, aur declaration aapko deni hoti hai — broker ko nahi.

In January 2024 the RBI issued a circular that came into force on 3 May 2024 (the original date of 5 April 2024 was deferred). Its meaning is direct: exchange-traded currency derivatives may be used only to hedge a valid underlying contracted exposure.

Up to the equivalent of USD 100 million across all currency pairs, a participant need not produce evidence of the exposure — but must confirm that such exposure exists.

In practice this segment is not a speculation arena. It is a hedging instrument. This market teaches it that way — because that is both the law and the truth.

Read your own broker's current onboarding and declaration process. Implementation differs a little between brokers, and the declaration is yours to make — not the broker's.

2

Pair padhnaReading a pair

Amaan

Ab basics. USDINR ka matlab hai: ek dollar kitne rupaye ka. Pehli currency base hai, doosri quote.

USDINR upar jaana matlab dollar mazboot, rupaya kamzor. Neeche jaana matlab ulta. Ek exporter ke liye USDINR upar jaana achhi khabar hai; importer ke liye buri.

Aur ek cheez jo equity wale log chaunk jaate hain: yahan move bahut chhote hote hain. Ek paisa — 0.01 rupaya — ek pura tick hai. Rupaya ek din mein aksar utna hi hilta hai jitna ek stock do minute mein.

Now the basics. USDINR means: how many rupees for one dollar. The first currency is the base, the second the quote.

USDINR going up means a stronger dollar and a weaker rupee. Down is the reverse. For an exporter, USDINR rising is good news; for an importer it isn't.

And something that surprises equity people: the moves here are tiny. One paisa — ₹0.01 — is a full tick. The rupee often moves as much in a day as a stock does in two minutes.

Arth

Toh phir paisa banta kaise hai?

So how does anyone make money?

Amaan

Leverage se. Aur wahi is segment ka sabse bada khatra hai. Chhota move, bada contract — toh chhoti si hulchal bhi bade rupaye ban jaati hai, dono taraf.

Par yaad rakho — mera maqsad paisa banana hai hi nahi. Mera maqsad apni invoice ki keemat pakki karna hai. Agar mera hedge break-even pe khatam ho, toh mere liye woh ek safal hedge hai.

Leverage. Which is this segment's biggest danger. A small move on a large contract — so a tiny wobble becomes a large rupee number, in either direction.

But remember — my aim isn't to make money. My aim is to fix the value of my invoice. If my hedge ends at break-even, for me that is a successful hedge.

3

Legality ki lineThe legality line

Amaan

Ab sabse zaroori baat, aur ise main footnote mein nahi chhodunga.

Legal kya hai: NSE aur BSE pe exchange-traded currency derivatives, RBI aur SEBI ke framework ke andar, underlying exposure ki declaration ke saath.

Legal kya nahi hai: offshore ya app-based FX aur CFD platforms pe rupee trading. Indian residents ke liye FEMA 1999 ke under woh permitted nahi hai. RBI unauthorised electronic trading platforms ki ek Alert List bhi publish karta hai.

Aur ek galat-fehmi jo bahut aam hai: LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme) videsh mein FX trading karne ki permission nahi hai. Woh ek alag cheez ke liye hai. Yeh do cheezein mila dena mehenga padta hai.

Now the most important point, and I won't leave it in a footnote.

What is legal: exchange-traded currency derivatives on NSE and BSE, inside the RBI and SEBI framework, with the underlying-exposure declaration.

What is not: trading FX on offshore or app-based FX/CFD platforms. That is not permitted for Indian residents under FEMA 1999. The RBI also publishes an Alert List of unauthorised electronic trading platforms.

And a very common misunderstanding: the LRS (Liberalised Remittance Scheme) is not permission to trade FX abroad. It exists for something else. Conflating the two gets expensive.

Tejas

People in my group trade on a forex app. So all of that…

Amaan

Pehle RBI ki Alert List dekho. Phir apne aap se woh sawaal poocho.

Look at the RBI's Alert List first. Then ask yourself that question.

RBI's managed float, reserves, the crude bill, FII flows, REER. And the shape of the Indian currency segment.

M3–4M3–4~35 min~35 min
1

Stock ka koi malik nahi hota. Rupaye ka hai.A stock has no owner. The rupee does.

Amaan

Equity aur currency mein ek buniyaadi farak hai jo log samajhte nahi.

Kisi stock ka koi aisa participant nahi hota jiske paas unlimited paisa ho aur jiska maqsad profit na ho. Rupaye ka hai — RBI.

RBI ke paas domestic currency unlimited hai aur uske paas bade forex reserves hain. Aur uska maqsad paisa kamana nahi hai — uska maqsad ateet ki ghabraahat rokna hai.

Isliye rupaya “free float” nahi hai. Woh ek managed float hai. Woh chalta apne aap hai, par ek hadd ke baad koi use rok deta hai.

There's a basic difference between equity and currency that people miss.

No stock has a participant with unlimited money and no profit motive. The rupee does — the RBI.

The RBI has unlimited domestic currency and large forex reserves. And its purpose isn't profit — its purpose is preventing disorderly moves.

Which is why the rupee isn't a free float. It's a managed float. It moves on its own, but past a point someone stops it.

Arth

Toh trend following yahan kaam nahi karegi?

So does trend following not work here?

Amaan

Kaam karti hai, par ek imaandaar sachchai ke saath: RBI ki smoothing baar-baar trend kaat deti hai. Rupaye ke bade trends aksar dheere aur lambe hote hain, tez aur saaf nahi.

It works, with one honest caveat: RBI smoothing repeatedly cuts trends short. The rupee's big trends tend to be slow and long rather than fast and clean.

In Chapter 32 we'll write a trend system with that caveat built into it.

2

Segment ka dhaanchaThe shape of the segment

Amaan

Ab practical baat. NSE aur BSE pe currency derivatives segment mein chaar mukhya pairs hain: USDINR, EURINR, GBPINR, JPYINR. In ke alawa kuch cross-currency contracts bhi hain — EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY.

Timings equity se alag hain — yeh segment equity ke band hone ke baad bhi chalta rehta hai. Exact timings apne broker aur exchange ki site se verify karo, kyunki yeh samay-samay pe badalti hain.

Aur sabse zaroori practical baat: yeh contracts cash settled hain, rupaye mein. Aapko kabhi dollar deliver nahi karne hote. Expiry pe sirf farak ka hisaab hota hai.

Now the practical part. The currency derivatives segment on NSE and BSE carries four main pairs: USDINR, EURINR, GBPINR, JPYINR. Alongside them are cross-currency contracts — EURUSD, GBPUSD, USDJPY.

The timings differ from equity — this segment keeps running after equity closes. Verify the exact timings on your broker's and the exchange's site, because they change from time to time.

And the most important practical point: these contracts are cash settled, in rupees. You never deliver dollars. At expiry only the difference is settled.

Arth

Toh Amaan, aapko apne asli dollar ka kya karna padta hai?

So Amaan, what do you do with your actual dollars?

Amaan

Woh alag raasta hai — bank ke through. Exchange pe mera hedge sirf rupaye mein settle hota hai aur mere nuksaan ki bharpaai karta hai. Do alag lenden hain, ek hi maqsad ke liye.

Yeh baat naye log sabse zyada galat samajhte hain. Hedge aur asli currency conversion do alag cheezein hain.

That's a separate route — through the bank. My exchange hedge settles in rupees and offsets the loss. Two separate transactions serving one purpose.

This is what beginners misunderstand most. The hedge and the actual currency conversion are two different things.

Expiry pe bhaav kaun tay karta haiWho sets the price at expiry

Amaan

Expiry pe ek sawaal uthta hai: settlement kis bhaav pe hoga? Exchange ka last traded price kaafi manipulable hota — isliye woh use nahi hota.

Charge stack — equity se alagThe charge stack — different from equity

Which means the hours between this segment's close and open are dark for you. Size an overnight position assuming anything could have happened in that dark.

That's three times normal. And it happens several times a year — on a Fed decision, a global shock, or when the RBI steps back.

Kam volatility mein options alag behave karte hainOptions behave differently in low volatility

Amaan

Equity options se aane pe currency options pehle sasta lagta hai. Woh sasta hai — par uski wajah samajhna zaroori hai.

Rupaya aise hi upar nahi jaataThe rupee's drift isn't an accident

Amaan

Ek baat jo har currency trader ko pehle din pata honi chahiye: USDINR ka lambe samay mein upar jaane ka jhukaav ek sanyog nahi hai. Woh ek ganit hai.

India ki interest rates aam taur pe US se zyada rahi hain. Aur jab do deshon ki rates alag hoti hain, toh unki currency ka forward rate spot se alag hota hai — is tarah ki koi bina risk ke paisa na bana sake.

Something every currency trader should know on day one: USDINR's long-run upward tilt is not an accident. It is arithmetic.

India's interest rates have generally run above the US's. When two countries' rates differ, their currency's forward rate differs from spot — in a way that stops anyone making riskless money.

This is interest rate parity, and the gap is the forward premium. Practically: USDINR futures usually quote above spot, and that gap shrinks toward expiry.

Arth

Toh long position rakhne wale ko woh farak apne aap milta hai?

So does a long position collect that gap automatically?

Amaan

Ulta. Long rakhne wale ko woh farak dena padta hai — woh spot se mehenga khareed raha hai. Short rakhne wale ko woh milta hai. Isi ko carry kehte hain.

Aur yahin ek ajeeb baat hai jo currency ko equity se alag banati hai: rupaya dheere-dheere kamzor ho toh short wale ko carry milta hai, par direction uske against jaati hai. Do ulti taakatein ek hi position mein.

The reverse. A long position pays that gap — it buys above spot. A short collects it. That is carry.

And here's the oddity that separates currency from equity: if the rupee weakens slowly, the short earns carry while direction runs against it. Two opposing forces in one position.

2

Iska practical matlab: spread chauda, depth kam, aur bade size mein nikalna mushkil. Aur isi liquidity ki wajah se yahan backtest asli se kahin behtar dikhte hain — woh Chapter 37 ka poora vishay hai.

Indian exchanges also list EURUSD, GBPUSD and USDJPY. There's no rupee in them — they're relationships between two foreign currencies, settled in rupees.

Now the honest part: their liquidity is very small against the global FX market. EURUSD globally is the most traded pair in the world. Its contract on an Indian exchange is a very small copy of it.

Practically: wider spreads, less depth, and difficulty exiting in size. And because of that liquidity, backtests here look far better than reality — which is Chapter 37's whole subject.

For everyone else, it's casting a large net in a small pond.

Currency BacktestBacktesting the Rupee

Saaf INR data kahan se, aur woh thin-liquidity trap jo har currency backtest ko sundar bana deta hai.

Where clean INR data comes from, and the thin-liquidity trap that flatters every currency backtest.

M19M19~30 min~30 min
1

Do data, do alag duniyaTwo data sets, two different worlds

Amaan

Currency backtest mein pehli galti data ke level pe hoti hai, strategy ke level pe nahi.

Do alag cheezein milti hain aur log unhe mila dete hain: spot rate (global interbank market ka rate, jo 24 ghante chalta hai) aur exchange ka futures data (jo sirf segment ke khulne ke waqt mein banta hai).

Aap spot data pe backtest karke exchange futures pe trade nahi kar sakte. Spot mein woh gap hai hi nahi jo aapko asli mein milega, aur usme carry bhi nahi hai.

The first error in a currency backtest happens at the data level, not the strategy level.

Two different things exist and get conflated: the spot rate (the global interbank rate, running 24 hours) and the exchange's futures data (which exists only during segment hours).

You cannot backtest on spot and trade exchange futures. Spot has none of the gaps you'll actually face, and none of the carry either.

Arth

Toh backtest kis data pe hona chahiye?

So which data should a backtest use?

Amaan

Us instrument ke data pe jise aap asal mein trade karoge — current-month futures, contract-dar-contract, rollover ke saath. Broker ki API se woh milta hai.

Aur contract-dar-contract jodte waqt rollover ka adjustment karna padta hai, warna aapke chart mein har expiry pe ek jhootha gap aa jaayega.

On the data for the instrument you'll actually trade — current-month futures, contract by contract, with rollovers. Your broker's API provides it.

And when stitching contracts together you must adjust for the roll, or your chart carries a false gap at every expiry.

Thin-liquidity trap — currency ka apna jhoothThe thin-liquidity trap — currency's own lie

Backtest maan leta hai ki aapko har bhaav pe fill mil gaya. USDINR mein yeh maan lena thoda theek hai, kyunki woh gehra hai. EURINR, GBPINR, JPYINR aur cross-currency contracts mein yeh maan lena bilkul galat hai.

Woh contracts patle hain. Aapke backtest ke aadhe trades asli mein fill hi nahi hote, aur jo hote hain woh bure bhaav pe hote hain. Nateeja: backtest jitna sundar hai, instrument utna hi patla hai — yeh ulta rishta currency mein bahut aam hai.

Iska ek hi imaandaar hal hai: apne backtest mein ek volume filter daalo. Agar us din us contract ka volume aapke size se kaafi bada nahi tha, toh woh trade ginti mein hi nahi aana chahiye.

A backtest assumes you got filled at every price. In USDINR that assumption is roughly acceptable, because it's deep. In EURINR, GBPINR, JPYINR and the cross-currency contracts it is simply wrong.

Those contracts are thin. Half your backtested trades wouldn't fill at all, and those that did would fill badly. The result: the prettier the backtest, the thinner the instrument — an inverse relationship that is very common in currency.

There is one honest fix: put a volume filter into the backtest itself. If that contract's volume that day didn't dwarf your size, the trade shouldn't count at all.

Managed Float Mein QuantQuant in a Managed Float

Jab ek participant ke paas unlimited paisa ho toh mean reversion aur momentum alag chalte hain.

How mean reversion and momentum change when one participant has unlimited money.

M20–21M20–21~40 min~40 min
1

Ek aisa khiladi jo haar nahi saktaA player who cannot be squeezed

Amaan

Quant models ek buniyaadi cheez maan ke chalte hain: saare participants ek jaise niyamon ke andar hain. Sabko margin lagti hai, sabka capital seemit hai, sab ek hadd ke baad position band karne pe majboor hain.

Rupaye mein woh maan-na tootta hai. RBI ke paas domestic currency unlimited hai. Uspe margin call nahi aata. Woh apni position kabhi majboori mein band nahi karta.

Iska matlab: rupaye mein bade extremes lagbhag nahi bante, aur jo bante hain woh natural nahi — woh tab bante hain jab RBI jaan-boojh kar peechhe hat jaata hai.

Quant models assume something basic: all participants face the same constraints. Everyone posts margin, everyone's capital is finite, everyone is eventually forced to close.

In the rupee that assumption breaks. The RBI's domestic currency is unlimited. It gets no margin call. It never closes a position under duress.

Which means: large extremes barely form in the rupee, and those that do aren't natural — they form when the RBI deliberately steps back.

Arth

Toh mean reversion yahan achhi chalni chahiye?

So mean reversion should work well here?

Amaan

Theory mein haan — aur practice mein ek shart ke saath. Mean reversion yahan chalta hai, par uska munafa bahut chhota hota hai, kyunki range hi tang hai. Aur chhota munafa Chapter 30 wale cost stack se takraata hai.

Momentum ka ulta maamla hai: woh yahan kam baar chalta hai, par jab chalta hai toh bada chalta hai — kyunki woh tabhi chalta hai jab RBI ne hatne ka faisla kiya ho.

In theory yes — and in practice with one caveat. Mean reversion does work here, but its profit is very small, because the range itself is tight. And small profit runs into Chapter 30's cost stack.

Momentum is the mirror image: it works less often here, but when it works it runs large — because it only works when the RBI has chosen to stand aside.

2

Tail events — unke pehle kya hua thaTail events — what preceded them

Amaan

Rupaye ke asli bade moves ginti ke hote hain. Aur agar aap unhe ginne baitho, toh ek pattern dikhta hai: woh lagbhag hamesha kisi bahari jhatke ke baad aate hain, aur unse pehle ek lamba shaant daur hota hai.

Yeh “lamba shaant daur phir achanak bada move” wala pattern currency ka sabse zaroori sabak hai. Kyunki shaant daur mein hi log size badhate hain — aur wahi size bade move ke din unhe le doobta hai.

Isliye mera regime filter ulta chalta hai: jitni lambi shaanti, utna chhota size. Yeh ulta lagta hai, aur yahi sahi hai.

The rupee's genuinely large moves are countable. And if you sit down and count them, a pattern shows: they almost always follow an external shock, and are preceded by a long quiet stretch.

That “long calm, then a sharp move” shape is currency's most important lesson. Because it's during the calm that people raise size — and that size is what sinks them on the sharp day.

So my regime filter runs inverted: the longer the calm, the smaller the size. It feels backwards, and it is correct.

Volatility regime — do number jo aapko bachate hainVolatility regimes — two numbers that save you

Ek — realised volatility ka percentile. Aaj ki 20-din realised volatility apne 3-saal ke itihaas mein kahan hai? Agar woh sabse neeche wale 10% mein hai, toh aap ek compression daur mein ho, aur woh daur hamesha khatam hota hai.

Do — India VIX aur USDINR ki implied volatility. Dono ek saath bahut neeche hon toh market kisi cheez ki ummeed hi nahi kar raha. Yeh aksar sabse mehenga waqt hota hai size badhane ka.

Yeh do number signal nahi hain. Yeh size ke inputs hain. Farak yaad rakho.

One — the percentile of realised volatility. Where does today's 20-day realised volatility sit in its three-year history? In the bottom 10%, you are in a compression stretch — and those always end.

Two — India VIX and USDINR implied volatility. Both very low together means the market expects nothing. That is usually the most expensive moment to increase size.

These two aren't signals. They are inputs to size. Keep the distinction.

M22–23M22–23~40 min~40 min
1

Spread — do positions, ek sochA spread — two positions, one idea

Amaan

Calendar spread ka matlab hai: ek mahine ka contract kharido, doosre ka becho. Aap bhaav pe bet nahi kar rahe — aap do mahinon ke farak pe bet kar rahe ho.

Aur woh farak, jaise Chapter 35 mein dekha, forward premium se banta hai. Toh calendar spread asal mein rate differential ki ummeed pe trade hai, rupaye ki direction pe nahi.

Iska ek practical faayda hai: exchange in dono legs ko ek saath dekhta hai aur recognised spread maan ke margin kam kar deta hai. Do alag positions ki margin se yeh kaafi kam padta hai.

A calendar spread means buying one month and selling another. You aren't betting on price — you're betting on the gap between two months.

And that gap, as Chapter 35 showed, comes from the forward premium. So a calendar spread is really a trade on expected rate differentials, not on the rupee's direction.

There's a practical benefit: the exchange sees both legs together and, as a recognised spread, reduces the margin. Far less than two separate positions would need.

Arth

Toh yeh safe hai?

So is it safe?

Amaan

Kam margin ka matlab kam risk nahi hota. Market 1 ka Chapter 14 yaad karo. Kam margin ka matlab hai ki exchange maanta hai ki dono legs ek doosre ko thoda cover karte hain.

Par woh cover tab tootta hai jab do mahinon ka rishta badal jaaye — aur woh badalta hai, khaas kar policy ke aas-paas. Aur kam margin ki wajah se log bada size le lete hain. Wahi asli khatra hai.

Lower margin doesn't mean lower risk. Recall Market 1's Chapter 14. Lower margin means the exchange accepts that the legs partly offset.

But that offset breaks when the relationship between the two months shifts — and it does, especially around policy. And low margin tempts people into larger size. That's the real danger.

2

Fair value — ek model jo “pata nahi” bol sakeFair value — a model that can say “I don't know”

Amaan

Ab ek fair-value model. Teen cheezein daalo: REER (real effective exchange rate — mehengai ke hisaab se adjust kiya hua rate), rate differential, aur oil ka import bill.

Model ka kaam yeh nahi hai ki woh kal ka bhaav bataye. Uska kaam yeh hai ki woh bataye ki aaj ka bhaav apne structural anchor se kitna door hai.

Aur sabse zaroori feature: model ko yeh bhi bolna aana chahiye ki “abhi koi view nahi hai”. Jo model roz ek jawab deta hai, woh model nahi — woh ek raay generator hai.

Now a fair-value model. Three inputs: REER (the real effective exchange rate — adjusted for inflation), the rate differential, and the oil import bill.

The model's job isn't to forecast tomorrow. Its job is to say how far today's price sits from its structural anchor.

And the most important feature: the model must be able to say “no view right now”. A model that produces an answer every day isn't a model — it's an opinion generator.

Hedger ke liye fair value ka asli istemaalWhat fair value is really for, to a hedger

Trader fair value ko entry ke liye use karta hai. Hedger use timing ke liye use karta hai — jo ek bilkul alag kaam hai.

Amaan ko hedge lagana hi hai; sawaal sirf yeh hai ki aaj lagaye ya do hafte baad. Agar model kehta hai ki bhaav anchor se kaafi door hai, toh woh thoda ruk sakta hai. Agar model “koi view nahi” kehta hai, toh woh aaj hi laga deta hai — kyunki intezaar ki koi wajah nahi hai.

A trader uses fair value for entry. A hedger uses it for timing — a different job entirely.

Amaan has to hedge regardless; the question is only today or in two weeks. If the model says price is far from the anchor, he can wait a little. If it says “no view”, he hedges today — because there's no reason to wait.
